# Twilio Voice AI and ConversationRelay: official-source findings (2026-10-14)

This note supports a **demo/MVP for learning Twilio Voice AI** and a production-style senior-engineer design exercise. It is not a claim that the candidate built Twilio's platform or shipped this design for a real customer. Platform facts below come from Twilio's current documentation. The appointment workflow, safeguards, and success measures are proposed application design unless explicitly identified as Twilio behavior.

## Business framing

Twilio describes Voice AI as a way to automate routine support requests, send reminders, and route complex calls to people with context. It explicitly advises teams to define the business opportunity, measure the current voice journey, decide which calls to automate, and measure return on investment. An **illustrative** appointment scheduling assistant could therefore aim to serve after-hours callers, resolve straightforward bookings and reschedules, reduce repetitive agent work, and preserve a useful human handoff for exceptions. There is no basis here for attributing any conversion, cost, completion, or latency result to this candidate or toy repository. [Twilio Voice AI use cases](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/use-cases/voice-AI)

## What Twilio provides versus what the application owns

| Layer | Documented Twilio behavior | Proposed application responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Call entry | Twilio requests a Voice URL for TwiML; `<Connect><ConversationRelay url="wss://...">` attaches the call to the WebSocket service. An optional `welcomeGreeting` is spoken after connection; an optional `<Connect action="...">` callback is requested when the session ends. [Onboarding](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/onboarding), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay) | Validate the inbound webhook; choose the caller journey, fallback, and transfer route. |
| Speech | ConversationRelay manages live speech-to-text (STT), text-to-speech (TTS), and real-time communication. The app receives transcribed speech and returns text, rather than handling raw call audio. [ConversationRelay reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay) | Run the LLM, manage prompts and context, authorize tools, apply business rules, record task and transaction state. |
| Events | Initial `setup` includes `sessionId`, `callSid`, caller metadata, and optional `customParameters`. Caller speech arrives as `prompt` with `voicePrompt`, `lang`, and `last`; `last` indicates the STT provider and session have determined the utterance is complete. Twilio may also send `dtmf`, `interrupt`, and `error`. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages) | Allocate one state record and generation owner per call/session; treat partial and final prompts according to configured STT behavior. |
| Responses | The app sends `text` messages with `token`, optional `last`, `interruptible`, and `preemptible`. Twilio turns text into speech. Twilio recommends streaming tokens as they are ready to reduce delay. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [best practices](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/best-practices) | Stream only the current turn's output; stop stale generation and prevent old tokens from being sent after a new turn or transfer. |

### Call walkthrough supported by the docs

1. A caller reaches the configured Twilio number. Twilio requests the application's Voice URL, whose TwiML returns `<Response><Connect action="https://.../connect-action"><ConversationRelay url="wss://.../ws" .../></Connect></Response>`. `url` must be `wss://`. [Onboarding](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/onboarding), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay)
2. The server validates the WebSocket handshake's `X-Twilio-Signature`, accepts the connection, then receives `setup` with `callSid` and `sessionId`. [Onboarding](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/onboarding), [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages)
3. Twilio transcribes the caller. The app receives a `prompt` containing `voicePrompt` and uses the final `last: true` event as its confirmed turn boundary. For certain Deepgram Flux configurations, `partialPrompts` can also provide provisional `last: false` prompts; any speculative work based on these needs application control. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay)
4. The app passes authorized context to its LLM. The LLM may request an application-defined booking tool; the backend validates identity, constraints, and state before calling the scheduling system. **This is proposed architecture, not a built-in ConversationRelay transaction feature.**
5. The app sends current-turn `text` tokens over the WebSocket, ending the talk cycle with `last: true`; Twilio synthesizes and plays them. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [best practices](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/best-practices)

## The critical interview tradeoffs

### ConversationRelay or Media Streams

ConversationRelay is a good fit when the team wants Twilio to manage call audio, STT, TTS, and turn-taking while its service works with text and business tools. [ConversationRelay reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay) Media Streams carries raw audio over WebSockets. A bidirectional Media Stream can receive inbound audio and send outbound audio, but the application must handle audio encoding and the chosen STT/TTS or speech-to-speech stack; Twilio documents outbound `media` as base64 `audio/x-mulaw` at 8 kHz and `mark`/`clear` playback control. Choose Media Streams when raw-audio access, an end-to-end speech model, or custom media processing is a requirement that justifies that operational work. The choice and last sentence are an architectural inference from Twilio's interfaces. [Media Streams overview](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams), [Media Streams WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams/websocket-messages)

### Interruption, generated text, and heard text

Twilio sends `interrupt` when speech interrupts TTS; it includes `utteranceUntilInterrupt` and `durationUntilInterruptMs`. `interruptible` controls whether caller input stops playback; `reportInputDuringAgentSpeech` separately controls whether the app receives input during agent speech. `events="tokens-played"` can report what was played. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay)

**Design recommendation:** on interruption, increment a per-call turn/generation epoch, cancel or drain the old LLM stream, discard late tokens, and update conversational history to reflect `utteranceUntilInterrupt` or playback events. Sent text and generated text are not proof that the caller heard them. Twilio stops interruptible playback, but cancellation of the LLM and history repair are application responsibilities. The initial `interrupt` can arrive before the next final caller `prompt`, so keep state transitions explicit.

### Tool execution and unknown scheduling outcomes

ConversationRelay's `interrupt` is a media/conversation event; the WebSocket protocol does not provide a rollback of a booking tool or external transaction. **Proposed design:** separate the speech-generation task from the booking operation. Once a reschedule request has been accepted by the scheduling service, interruption or transfer must not cause an unexamined retry or an assertion that the change failed. If an API call times out, mark the outcome `unknown`; query the authoritative booking state by an operation or request ID. Only retry safely if the scheduling backend supports duplicate protection for the *same* operation ID and its semantics are understood. Otherwise transfer the unresolved case for human review. Do not tell the caller a booking is confirmed until the scheduling service confirms it. These are general distributed-systems safeguards, not Twilio guarantees.

### Simultaneous calls and disconnection

`setup` identifies `callSid` and `sessionId`; a new ConversationRelay session can be associated with the same `callSid`. The action callback includes both identifiers and session status. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay) **Proposed design:** keep per-call conversation state keyed by `callSid`, per-connection state keyed by `sessionId`, and each stream/tool task tied to its call, turn epoch, and operation ID. Clean up ephemeral tasks on disconnect; persist enough transaction state to reconcile an in-flight booking after the socket is gone. Never let one call's prompt or result reach another call's socket.

Twilio explicitly says it **does not automatically reconnect** an unexpectedly lost ConversationRelay WebSocket and reports a failed session. The same page describes returning new `<Connect><ConversationRelay>` TwiML from the `<Connect>` `action` callback to create a replacement session and verifying the `callSid`. The docs leave some ambiguity about whether a specific failure has already disconnected the **call** versus only the **session**, so do not promise automatic uninterrupted recovery; test the failure path with the chosen Twilio configuration and provide a fallback such as apology and human routing where the call remains alive. [WebSocket messages, limitations and reconnection](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [TwiML callback statuses](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay)

### Human handoff and the combined race

The app can send `{ "type": "end", "handoffData": "..." }` to end the ConversationRelay session. `handoffData` is a string returned as `HandoffData` in the `<Connect>` action callback; the callback can return TwiML to route to a human with `<Dial>` or a queue. Do not put PCI data in `handoffData`. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages), [TwiML reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay), [Twilio transfer example](https://www.twilio.com/docs/conversation-intelligence-classic/conversation-relay-integration), [Dial reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/dial)

**Proposed behavior for “booking API times out, caller interrupts, success arrives during transfer”:** stop the old spoken response and any stale tokens; tell the caller only the last *confirmed* state, for example that the system is checking the change and connecting them to an agent. Preserve one operation ID and reconcile the late success against the authoritative booking record. Before handoff, persist a compact record for the agent: verified identity status, requested old/new appointment, operation ID, most recent authoritative result, whether the caller heard a confirmation, and any uncertainty still unresolved. When success arrives, atomically update that record; the agent must refresh it on answer, because `HandoffData` is a one-time callback payload and can become stale. If confirmed before the agent answers, the agent sees “confirmed, caller may not have heard it” and can tell the caller; if still unknown, the agent sees “pending verification” and checks before promising or retrying. This is application design, not a Twilio-provided handoff state machine.

### Security and measures

Twilio says to validate the initial WebSocket `X-Twilio-Signature` with the auth token and request URL. Its webhook security guide recommends SDK validation using the exact URL and all request parameters; it warns that parameter sets can evolve. Validate both the Voice webhook and `<Connect>` action callback as well. [Onboarding](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/onboarding), [Secure webhooks](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/webhooks/webhooks-security) **Application design:** authenticate the customer independently of the phone number, authorize the requested appointment, restrict tool permissions, keep secrets out of prompts/logs, and limit transcript access and retention. Twilio identity of the WebSocket connection is not customer authorization.

Twilio Conversation Relay Insights breaks response time into network RTT, STT, application time to first token, and TTS to start of playback; its events mark first/final token and start/end of customer/agent speech. Its measurements exclude last-mile network latency, and STT estimates vary by provider/language. [Call summary](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/voice-insights/conversation-relay-summary), [Insights events](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/voice-insights/api/call/details-conversation-relay-events) **Proposed test and business metrics:** completion rate with correct authoritative bookings, duplicate or incorrect mutations, unknown-outcome resolution time, transfer reasons, abandonment, interruption recovery, time to first audible reply, p50/p95 latency by stage, and cost per correctly completed request. Inject STT partial/final races, caller interruption, late LLM tokens, booking timeouts with late success, concurrent calls, failed WebSocket, and handoff races. Keep actual measured results separate from target values.

## Documentation cautions

- Twilio's current WebSocket reference uses `type: "end"` for session completion. Some older tutorials use different wording or examples; implement against the current reference. [WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages)
- ConversationRelay is documented as HIPAA eligible **when configured properly** and with a signed BAA for applicable healthcare workflows; Conversation Relay **Insights** pages explicitly say that Insights is not HIPAA eligible. Do not imply the whole proposed appointment product is compliant merely because ConversationRelay can be configured for it. [ConversationRelay reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay), [Insights summary](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/voice-insights/conversation-relay-summary)
- Twilio's marketing use-case examples and customer outcome numbers describe other organizations, not this candidate or repository. [Voice AI use cases](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/use-cases/voice-AI)
