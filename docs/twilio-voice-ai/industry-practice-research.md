# Production voice AI: answering caller questions and follow-ups

Research date: 2026-10-15. This is a source-grounded design reference for a possible production application, not a description of the demo's implemented behavior or of the author's employment.

## Recommended interaction pattern

1. **Treat every utterance as a turn in one call session.** Twilio ConversationRelay delivers transcribed `prompt` messages, with `last` indicating completion, over the call WebSocket. The application should keep the current intent, verified identity status, relevant entities (such as which appointment or policy the caller means), and what the caller actually heard. For a follow-up such as “Is it free?”, use the recent turn and retrieved source to resolve “it”; ask a short clarification if there are multiple plausible referents. The per-call state design is an application recommendation, while the `prompt` and interruption fields are defined by [Twilio's WebSocket protocol](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages). Google's [data-store guidance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/data-store/settings) explicitly recommends including the conversation with sources to answer contextual questions.
2. **Route by information type before answering.** Stable public policy or FAQ questions should use approved, maintained knowledge; live account, availability, pricing, or booking questions should call the authoritative backend after any required identity check. Ground the answer in returned facts and say when the source lacks the answer. Google's [data-store settings](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/data-store/settings) show a source-only answer and a `NOT_ENOUGH_INFORMATION` fallback. Microsoft's [voice-agent guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice) says to verify before disclosure, return only what tools provide, and avoid fabricated confirmations. The exact routing, authorization, and retrieval architecture are application choices.
3. **Answer for the ear.** Give the direct answer first, usually in one or two sentences, and ask at most one needed follow-up question. Omit repeated context, long lists, markup, raw URLs, and unrelated information. Speak dates and numbers naturally; confirm critical values before consequential actions. This is consistent across [Google's voice-agent design guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/voice-agent-design) and [Microsoft's voice-agent best practices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice). A short response is a design target, not a guarantee of factuality.
4. **Repair uncertainty in the conversation.** If speech recognition is uncertain, clarify the specific field rather than repeating a generic error. If the source has no answer or a tool fails, state the limitation briefly and offer an appropriate next step or person. Limit repeated no-match/no-input loops; Google recommends human escalation by the third such event in its Dialogflow voice design guide. See [Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/voice-agent-design) and [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice). A numeric escalation threshold is configurable policy, not a universal requirement.
5. **Handle interruption as a new conversational turn.** Twilio sends an `interrupt` message with `utteranceUntilInterrupt`, which reports the part played before barge-in. Stop obsolete generation and suppress late tokens in the application; update conversation history to what was heard, then process the new caller request. These cancellation and state-management steps are implementation recommendations inferred from [Twilio's interrupt protocol](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages) and [Microsoft's interruption guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice). An interruption of speech must not be treated as undoing a backend action.
6. **Stream where the risk allows it, then measure the audible experience.** Twilio says most applications should stream LLM text tokens to ConversationRelay as they arrive to reduce delay, but also notes that waiting for a complete answer can improve pacing and should be tested by use case. For fact-sensitive or transactional statements, validate the facts before speaking a confirmation; small validated chunks can then be streamed. Measure WebSocket network, STT, application, and TTS components, plus caller/agent interruption rates and actual task outcome. See [Twilio best practices](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/best-practices), [ConversationRelay call summary metrics](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/voice-insights/conversation-relay-summary), and [Microsoft's evaluation guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice).
7. **Make handoff an outcome with context.** Escalate for a caller request, unsupported or sensitive issue, failed verification, repeated misunderstanding, or unresolved tool failure. Say what will happen, and give the receiving agent the caller's verified context, question, sources used, and any pending transaction's status. Never claim the transfer or business action succeeded until the respective system confirms it. See [Microsoft's handoff guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice) and [Google's voice design guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/voice-agent-design). The precise handoff payload and transaction reconciliation are application design.

## Example follow-up

Caller: “Do you have parking?” Agent: “Yes, parking is available at the clinic.” Caller: “Is it free?” Agent: “Let me check the parking policy.” After retrieving the current policy, answer only the fee question, e.g. “Yes, parking is free,” **if the source explicitly says so**. Otherwise: “I don't have a confirmed parking fee. I can connect you with the front desk.” The example is illustrative; it makes no claim about any real clinic policy.

## Evaluation cases

Test multi-turn references (“it”, “that appointment”), topic switches, ambiguous references, unsupported questions, outdated FAQ documents, live-data changes, identity failures, noisy speech, interruptions at several points, tool errors, and transfer during pending actions. Review real call audio alongside transcripts because text-only evaluation misses acoustic and timing failures. Track grounded answer correctness, task completion and first-contact resolution, transfer reasons, repeated no-match, latency to first audible response, interruption rate, and cost per correctly resolved call. These measures combine [Google's suggested voice-agent quality metrics](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/voice-agent-design), [Twilio's call metrics](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/voice-insights/conversation-relay-summary), and [Microsoft's testing guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/voice-agent-best-practice).

## Important limits and tensions

- ConversationRelay supplies transcription, synthesis, and message events; it does **not** supply an application knowledge base, business authorization, or a guarantee that an LLM answer is correct. Those need explicit application controls. [Twilio protocol](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages).
- Streaming improves perceived speed but can expose an unverified answer before a complete-answer check. Gate claims that depend on a backend transaction or uncertain retrieval, and test the pacing/latency tradeoff. [Twilio best practices](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/best-practices).
- Vendor guidance differs in implementation detail and often describes vendor-specific products. The cross-vendor pattern above is a synthesis, not a formal industry standard.
